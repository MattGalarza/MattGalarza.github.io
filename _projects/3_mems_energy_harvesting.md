## Overview

My doctoral research develops nonlinear electrostatic MEMS energy harvesters for low-frequency ambient vibration. I study how mechanical motion, variable capacitance, electrical loading, and nonlinear coupling interact to determine bandwidth, power, efficiency, and operating stability.

## Research directions

- Broadband and frequency-upconversion architectures
- Electromechanical coupling and energy-conversion pathways
- Resonance, bifurcation, impact, and pull-in dynamics
- Load matching and power-efficiency tradeoffs
- Geometry, topology, and parameter optimization

## Engineering objective

The broader aim is to translate nonlinear-dynamics insight into robust design rules for small autonomous sensors and other systems where battery replacement is difficult.
