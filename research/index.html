research

Research in nonlinear dynamics, energy harvesting, scientific machine learning, and thermal transport.

Nonlinear dynamics and electromechanical energy harvesting

My doctoral research focuses on coupled nonlinear electromechanical systems that convert ambient vibration into electrical energy. I study how coupling, damping, nonlinearity, and system architecture govern energy exchange, resonance, stability, and conversion performance.

The work combines Lagrangian, Hamiltonian, and Newtonian formulations with eigenvalue and eigenmode analysis, bifurcation analysis, numerical continuation, and time-domain simulation. A central goal is to develop physically interpretable design principles for broadband, low-frequency energy harvesting systems.

Selected themes

  • Energy transfer and conversion in coupled mechanical-electrical oscillators
  • Nonlinear response, stability, bifurcations, and impact dynamics
  • Modal structure, mixing, and entropy-inspired measures of energy redistribution
  • Energy-based and port-Hamiltonian descriptions of multiphysics systems
  • Topology and optimization of electrostatic MEMS energy harvesters

Data-driven modeling of physical systems

I use scientific machine learning to complement first-principles models when important dynamics are uncertain or unresolved. My work includes sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy), neural ordinary differential equations, universal differential equations, and Bayesian optimization.

These methods are used as physics-informed diagnostic and modeling tools: the objective is not only prediction, but also the discovery of interpretable corrections, latent interactions, and improved engineering designs.

Thermal transport in semiconductor thin films

My thermal transport research investigates the properties of semiconductor back-end-of-line materials and multilayer thin films. I fabricate and characterize test structures and use the 3-omega electrothermal method to estimate in-plane and cross-plane thermal conductivity.

This work includes cleanroom processing, photolithography, e-beam evaporation, liftoff, automated data reduction, sensitivity analysis, and inverse parameter estimation. The broader objective is to improve thermal characterization and modeling for next-generation semiconductor packaging and three-dimensional integration.

Engineering translation

I have also worked on robotics-enabled inspection and dimensional metrology for aerospace systems. At GE Aerospace Research, I developed compact inspection concepts, compliant mechanical interfaces, point-cloud processing workflows, and deployment documentation for in-situ engine inspection. This experience informs my interest in translating analytical research into robust tools that operate under real geometric, sensing, and manufacturing constraints.
