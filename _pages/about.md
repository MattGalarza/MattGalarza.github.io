I am an Aerospace Engineering PhD candidate at [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://www.rpi.edu/). My research examines how energy is transferred, converted, and dissipated in coupled nonlinear systems, with a particular focus on electromechanical energy harvesters.

My work combines nonlinear dynamics, applied mathematics, control theory, and scientific machine learning. I use Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics, stability and modal analysis, sparse system identification, neural differential equations, and numerical optimization to develop interpretable models of complex physical systems.

I also study thermal transport in semiconductor thin films using the 3-omega method, including the identification of in-plane and cross-plane thermal properties. Across these projects, I am interested in connecting first-principles physics with data-driven tools to improve both understanding and engineering design.

I received my B.S. and M.Eng. degrees in Mechanical Engineering from Rensselaer and expect to complete my PhD in December 2026. My broader engineering experience includes robotics-enabled inspection and metrology at GE Aerospace Research, robotics development at Collaborative Robotics, process engineering at Tesla, and project engineering at Sanofi Pasteur.

## Research interests

- Nonlinear and coupled dynamical systems
- Electromechanical vibration energy harvesting
- Hamiltonian and energy-based modeling
- Scientific machine learning and system identification
- Semiconductor thin-film thermal transport
- Robotics-enabled inspection and metrology
